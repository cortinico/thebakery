What changes when AI can take care of the tedious parts of building software, and help you try ideas that once felt out of reach?

Today we’re joined by [**Ivan Morgillo**](https://ivanmorgillo.com/), an Android engineer, indie developer, and longtime tinkerer. Ivan explains why he treats AI as an assistant for the annoying work, from wiring up [Home Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/) to managing a fleet of coding sessions from his phone.

We get into his agent workflow: using Claude Code as an entry point, asking several models to review plans and code, and building [**ship-feature**](https://github.com/hamen/ship-feature) to coordinate the process. Ivan also describes how he added persistent memory with [**MemPalace**](https://github.com/MemPalace/mempalace), originally to keep a long-running Dungeons & Dragons campaign going across conversations.

The conversation ranges from jailbreaking a Kindle and using a webcam as a feedback loop, to custom hardware controls, fixing memory leaks in the Elgato Stream Deck app, and asking AI to "clean" a Chromecast. We finish with indie app ideas, the story behind Ivan’s [Smart Pantry](https://nowastefood.app/) app, and his advice to live an interesting life so you encounter interesting problems to solve.
